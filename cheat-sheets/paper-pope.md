Sola scriptura is the Protestant doctrine that scripture alone is the authority for Christian faith and practice. Catholics, though, reject sola scriptura because they regard scripture, tradition, and the teaching authority of the Church (i.e., Magisterium) as co-authoritative. But is sola scriptura actually supported by the Bible? To answer that we need to first step back and consider a few additional ones:

<p style="margin-bottom: 0;">Haven't most Christians throughout time had their own copy of the Bible?</p>
<ul>
  <li>The Bible as we know it didn't exist until hundreds of years after the birth of the church.</li>
  <li>It took several centuries to finalize exactly what would be included in the New Testament (i.e., its "canon").</li>
  <li>Even after the New Testament canon was unofficially settled in the year 367, every copy of the Bible had to be handwritten, so almost all Bibles were confined to churches and monasteries.</li>
  <li>It would take more than a thousand <i>additional</i> years for regular people to obtain a Bible for themselves.</li>
</ul>

<p style="margin-bottom: 0;">Even so, didn't God intend for all Christians to <i>eventually</i> interpret the Bible by themselves?</p>
<ul>
<li>Widespread Bible availability created a new problem: Ordinary people could suddenly use scripture to create their own personal doctrines and dogmas.</li>
<li>The New Testament does not contain a systematic process for resolving textual disputes; there are no modern apostles, binding councils, courts, or judges.</li>
<li>Complicated and confusing scriptures + personal interpretation = a fractured religion</li>
</ul>

<p style="margin-bottom: 0;">But didn't all of the New Testament authors write exactly what God inspired them to?</p>
<ul>
<li>Actually, the New Testament's most prolific writer had a chronic credibility problem:
  <ul>
  <li>The apostle Paul explicitly asserted he would be alive for Jesus' return.</li>
  <li>Paul's radical doctrine of "righteousness by faith alone" was directly refuted by James, Jesus' own brother and the leader of the original church in Jerusalem.</li>
  <li>Paul said he publicly rebuked Peter in front of the church in Antioch, but also had to admit that even his closest ally Barnabas sided with Peter.</li>
  </ul>
</li>
</ul>

<p style="margin-bottom: 0;"><strong>Bottom line:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>For the first ~70% of Christian history, Sola Scriptura was impossible.</li>
  <li>It has caused Christianity to splinter into countless denominations (and sub-denominations), each claiming scriptural authority.</li>
  <li>A large portion of the New Testament was written by a false prophet (according to Deut. 18:17-22) – a man who was also in conflict with the original apostles throughout his entire ministry.</li>
</ul>
